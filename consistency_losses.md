# Efficient consistency training for trajectory reward models

This note describes how to train a shared sequence model to agree across two orderings: predict a trajectory with reward given, or predict reward after observing a trajectory prefix. Two teacher-forced passes provide all quantities needed for local, all-interval, and mixed consistency objectives. The exact mean squared error over every interval can be computed in linear time using a cumulative sum and a variance.

The identities and computational shortcuts below are exact under the stated assumptions. Loss weights, normalization choices, and training recommendations are experimental choices, not guarantees of improved optimization or policy performance.

## 2 The interval identity

For any 0 â‰¤ i < j â‰¤ n, Bayes consistency requires

$$
p_\theta(x_{i+1:j}\mid h_i,R)q_i(R)
=p_\theta(x_{i+1:j}\mid h_i)q_j(R).
$$

Both expressions describe the conditional joint probability of the continuation block and R given háµ¢. Define gathered log probabilities

$$
u_t=\log p_\theta(x_t\mid h_{t-1}),\qquad
v_t=\log p_\theta(x_t\mid h_{t-1},R),\qquad
b_t=\log q_t(R).
$$

The log residual for an interval is

$$
\Delta_{i,j}=\sum_{t=i+1}^{j}(v_t-u_t)+b_i-b_j.
$$

The one-step residual and its cumulative sum are

$$
\delta_t=v_t-u_t+b_{t-1}-b_t,\qquad
c_0=0,\quad c_k=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\delta_t.
$$

Their usefulness comes from changing gradient weighting and credit assignment, not from creating independent observations.

## 3 Loss choices

### Local one-step loss

$$
L_{\rm local}=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\delta_t^2.
$$

This directly penalizes each adjacent ordering disagreement. It requires O(n) work and has a simple interpretation. Long-range drift is penalized only through the individual local errors that produce it.

### Exact all-interval loss

$$
L_{\rm all}=\frac{1}{\binom{n+1}{2}}\sum_{i<j}\Delta_{i,j}^2.
$$

Let m = n+1 and cÌ„ = (1/m)Î£â‚–câ‚–. The elementary identity

$$
\sum_{i<j}(c_j-c_i)^2
=m\sum_{k=0}^n(c_k-\bar c)^2
$$

gives

$$
\boxed{L_{\rm all}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\operatorname{Var}(c_0,\ldots,c_n)}.
$$

Use population variance with denominator n+1. This computes exactly the same scalar and gradients as explicitly forming every pair. It requires O(n) work and storage beyond model evaluation. The shortcut applies to uniform interval weighting and squared residuals; arbitrary pair weights, clipping, or a Huber penalty do not generally preserve it.

The all-interval objective responds strongly to errors with the same sign across many steps. For example, if every Î´â‚œ = Îµ, then L_local = ÎµÂ² while L_all = (n+1)(n+2)ÎµÂ²/6. Alternating positive and negative errors partly cancel over long intervals, motivating a retained local term.

### Balanced local and all-interval mixture

$$
L_{\rm mix}=(1-\alpha)L_{\rm local}
+\alpha\frac{L_{\rm all}}{s(n)},\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq1.
$$

Choose s(n)=1 to preserve the raw all-interval objective, especially for fixed horizons. An optional scale adjustment is s(n)=(n+2)/3. Under hypothetical independent, zero-mean local residuals with common variance ÏƒÂ², E[L_all]=(n+2)ÏƒÂ²/3, so this makes its expected scale match L_local. Real residuals are correlated; this is a comparison convention, not a statistical model of training.

Start with Î±=0.5 as a baseline, and compare Î±=0 and Î±=1 using the same normalization convention. The overall consistency coefficient still needs tuning. This normalization does not make gradients equal in magnitude or remove the preference against coherent drift.

### Equal weighting across length scales

For more control, choose lengths D = {1,2,4,8,â€¦} up to n. For each length â„“, average over all valid starting points:

$$
L_\ell=\frac{1}{n-\ell+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-\ell}(c_{i+\ell}-c_i)^2,
\qquad
L_{\rm scales}=\frac1{|D|}\sum_{\ell\in D}\frac{L_\ell}{\ell}.
$$

Division by â„“ is optional; it equalizes expected squared residuals under the same independent-error heuristic. Without it, longer spans have more influence. Equal weight per scale prevents the number of intervals at a given length from setting its weight. Optionally add length n to include the full trajectory, avoiding a duplicate when n is already a power of two.

This uses O(n log n) arithmetic. A direct implementation may also retain O(n log n) intermediates for backward. Sampling a length and a start from the desired weighting gives an unbiased stochastic estimate, but is unnecessary for the linear-time uniform all-interval objective.

## 4 Obtaining the inputs efficiently

Run both modes with the same teacher-forced trajectory. Stack them in the batch dimension when memory permits, or run them sequentially and backpropagate their combined loss. Stacking is two examples' worth of model work, not a free second ordering. Changing the leading reward token changes later hidden states, so a standard transformer cannot reuse the NOR pass's later KV cache exactly.

For tensors padded to N tokens:

- u and v have shape [B,N], with gathered log probabilities of the actual next tokens.
- b has shape [B,N+1], with the observed or selected reward's log probability at every prefix, including the empty prefix.
- lengths has shape [B], counting valid trajectory tokens, including EOS if modeled.

For input [MODE,xâ‚,â€¦,xâ‚™], next-token logits at positions 0,â€¦,nâˆ’1 score xâ‚,â€¦,xâ‚™. NOR reward logits at positions 0,â€¦,n score R at hâ‚€,â€¦,hâ‚™. Compute log_softmax in float32 and gather the target class; do not multiply probabilities across a block and then take a logarithm.

Separate state and action heads are compatible with this construction: gather from the appropriate normalized head at each token position. For a continuous state head, use its actual log density in both modes, under the same reference measure. An arbitrary state reconstruction MSE cannot be substituted for a log likelihood without a specified probabilistic model.

## 5 PyTorch reference implementation

This function returns per-trajectory values so the caller can choose how to weight examples. It assumes right padding, finite gathered log probabilities on valid entries, lengths from 1 through N, and reward predictions at every prefix. No gradients are detached.

```python
import torch

def consistency_losses(u, v, b, lengths):
    # u, v: [B, N]; b: [B, N+1]; lengths: [B]
    # Model log_softmax should already have been computed in float32.
    u, v, b = u.float(), v.float(), b.float()
    B, N = u.shape
    lengths = lengths.to(device=u.device, dtype=torch.long)
    edge = torch.arange(N, device=u.device)[None, :] < lengths[:, None]
    node = torch.arange(N + 1, device=u.device)[None, :] <= lengths[:, None]

    # Replace unused entries before doing arithmetic.
    u = u.masked_fill(~edge, 0.0)
    v = v.masked_fill(~edge, 0.0)
    b = b.masked_fill(~node, 0.0)
    delta = (v - u + b[:, :-1] - b[:, 1:]).masked_fill(~edge, 0.0)
    c = torch.cat([torch.zeros_like(delta[:, :1]), delta.cumsum(-1)], -1)

    n = lengths.to(u.dtype)
    m = n + 1
    local = delta.square().sum(-1) / n
    mean = c.masked_fill(~node, 0.0).sum(-1) / m
    centered = (c - mean[:, None]).masked_fill(~node, 0.0)
    variance = centered.square().sum(-1) / m
    all_intervals = 2 * m / n * variance
    all_scaled = all_intervals / ((n + 2) / 3)
    return {
        "local": local,
        "all": all_intervals,
        "all_scaled": all_scaled,
        "c": c,
    }


def multiscale_loss(c, lengths):
    # Equal weight over valid power-of-two lengths for each example.
    # Includes length 1, and divides each squared interval residual by length.
    B, M = c.shape
    N = M - 1
    lengths = lengths.to(device=c.device, dtype=torch.long)
    total = c.new_zeros(B)
    count = c.new_zeros(B)
    ell = 1
    while ell <= N:
        starts = torch.arange(M - ell, device=c.device)[None, :]
        valid = starts + ell <= lengths[:, None]
        diff = (c[:, ell:] - c[:, :-ell]).masked_fill(~valid, 0.0)
        terms = valid.sum(-1)
        per_length = diff.square().sum(-1) / terms.clamp_min(1) / ell
        total = total + per_length
        count = count + (terms > 0).to(c.dtype)
        ell *= 2
    return total / count.clamp_min(1)
```

## 6 Supervised training and gradient flow

For an observed trajectory and its actual reward, a useful data objective is

$$
L_{\rm data}=
-\frac1n\sum_tu_t
-\beta\frac1n\sum_tv_t
-\gamma\frac1{n+1}\sum_{t=0}^nb_t.
$$

This combines unconditional token likelihood, reward-conditioned token likelihood, and reward supervision at every prefix. It is a composite supervised objective, not one uniquely defined joint log likelihood. Normalize each component explicitly so extra reward-head positions or longer sequences do not silently determine its weight.

Train with L_total = L_data + Î»_cons L_cons. Average the per-trajectory consistency loss across the batch for equal trajectory weighting. Pooling all pairs across a batch instead would give longer examples quadratically more weight.

Initially allow gradients into both token orderings and the NOR reward head. Neither side of an interval identity is presumed to be a correct teacher. Consistency alone can be reduced by making incorrect predictions agree; keep data supervision and monitor held-out likelihood. Warming up likelihood training or gradually increasing Î»_cons is a reasonable stabilization experiment.

If one branch is known exactly, such as an explicitly fixed behavior distribution, it can be held fixed. Stop-gradient elsewhere changes the update rule and should be an explicit ablation. The variance shortcut reproduces the fully differentiated squared pair loss; arbitrary teacher-target detachment applied separately to interval factors is not automatically equivalent to detaching parts of this shortcut.

Choose Î»_cons with a small sweep, using gradient norms on shared parameters as a diagnostic. Equal numerical loss values do not mean equal optimization influence. Squared log residuals are not log likelihoods or KL divergences, so this construction does not remove the need to choose a relative weight.

## 7 RL details that affect correctness

**Include stochastic state tokens.** For a block containing both actions and states, its likelihood includes both. Reward conditioning generally changes the posterior over stochastic outcomes, so p(Sâ‚œâ‚Šâ‚ âˆ£ hâ‚œ,Aâ‚œ,R) cannot generally be replaced by the unconditioned transition law. Deterministic known transitions can cancel on valid paths. Otherwise an action-only likelihood combined with a reward prediction after observing the next state is not the stated block identity.

**Choose boundaries deliberately.** If reward predictions are available only at full environment-step boundaries, sum token log ratios within each complete block and use the reward logits at those boundaries. Apply the same formulas to that shorter sequence of blocks. This enforces block consistency, not every internal token identity. An action-only identity is valid when both reward endpoints are immediately before and immediately after that action, before the next random state is observed.

**Keep task and policy semantics fixed.** These probabilities describe a joint trajectory distribution under the data-generating behavior policy or policy mixture, unless policy is included in the conditioning context. Consistency does not turn reward-conditioned sampling into an expected-return-optimal controller in a stochastic environment.

**Use supported reward conditions.** Start with the observed R, which requires one conditioned pass per example. A NOR pass supplies all K reward logits but does not supply trajectory likelihoods under all K reward conditions; each additional condition generally needs another conditioned pass. Never apply supervised trajectory likelihood to a deliberately mismatched reward as if it were observed data.

**Handle exact zeros separately.** The log residual assumes positive factors. A complete trajectory may determine its return exactly, making other reward labels impossible. For such a mismatched label, the original probability identity can have zero on both sides and the log form is undefined. Do not impose the finite-log squared loss across those exact support boundaries. Restrict to feasible trajectory-reward pairs or explicitly design a separate support-aware objective. Softmax positivity does not make an impossible event a valid target.

**Anchor terminal rewards.** When the complete recorded trajectory determines R, supervise the final NOR reward prediction on that actual R. Do not force qâ‚™(R)=1 when the recorded sequence omits information needed to determine it. Likewise, a truncated rollout is not a terminal trajectory merely because it reaches a batch length limit.

**Control dropout noise.** Different dropout masks introduce ordering disagreement even when deterministic predictions agree. Use zero dropout for the clean initial experiment, or measure its contribution and use deterministic evaluation. Sharing masks where practical is another option.
